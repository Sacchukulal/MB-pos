# Tax audit — the whole of GST in Magic Bill, 2026-08-24

Written on the owner's instruction: *"do a deep complete tax audit, find out how
tax should really needs to be calculated in India and what is currently
happening in our pos app and how to fix it — billing, print, report, menu,
settings, everywhere."*

**No code was changed.** This file is the **evidence** — what is wrong, and how
it was proved.

**The build plan is a separate file: `docs/TAX_REWORK_PROMPT.md` (P33).** It is
phase by phase, from `mb-core` outward, with the types, the migration, the tests
and the deletions written out. Two files rather than one, because that is what
P32 did: the audit is what was found, the prompt is what to do about it.

Every claim about the app carries a file and a line, and was checked by reading
the code, not by remembering it. Every claim about the law carries a source at
the end.

---

## 0. The short answer

**Your instinct was right, but not for the reason you gave.**

You said the tax section in Settings conflicts with the tax on a menu item, and
that the menu item is enough. Here is what is actually there:

| Settings → Tax | What it does today |
|---|---|
| **Tax rate for a new item** | **Nothing. Dead.** Declared, drawn on screen, never read by any code. |
| **Menu prices already include tax** | **Nothing. Dead.** Same — and it is the direct duplicate of a setting that *does* work on the tax class. |
| I am under the composition scheme | Changes the bill's title only. **Does not stop tax being charged**, which makes the bill illegal. |
| Where you usually supply | Real, and **dangerous** — see §3.6. |

Six mentions of those two dead settings exist in the entire repository. All six
are the declaration itself and the row that draws it. Nothing reads them:

```
src-tauri/src/settings/mod.rs:425      pub default_class_id: String,
src-tauri/src/settings/mod.rs:427      pub prices_include_tax: bool,
src-tauri/src/settings/catalog.rs:746  the row that draws the first
src-tauri/src/settings/catalog.rs:749  the row that draws the second
```

So the confusion you feel is real and it is not subtle: **the Settings tax
screen shows you two switches that do nothing.** You flip them and the app
ignores you.

But "remove tax from Settings entirely" is the wrong fix, because **three
different questions are being called "tax"** and only one of them belongs on a
menu item:

1. **What rate does this thing attract?** → belongs to the item. Already right.
2. **Is the price I typed tax-in or tax-on-top?** → belongs to the item too, and
   your objection is what proved it. See §2.3.
3. **What kind of taxpayer is this shop?** → belongs to Settings, and it is the
   part that is half-built and printing illegal bills.

The rule to hold on to: **Settings owns who the shop IS. The menu owns what
things ARE. Settings must never own a rate.**

## 1. Two things you said that I have to correct

You asked me to research properly and tell you. Two of the premises in your
message are wrong, and building on them would produce illegal bills, so I am
saying so plainly.

### 1.1 "There is IGST and State GST which is half SGST and half CGST"

**Nearly right, but they are alternatives, not a pair.** There is one GST rate
on a supply. How it is *named* depends on where the supply happens:

* **Inside your own state** → the rate splits in half. 5% becomes **CGST 2.5% +
  SGST 2.5%**. Two lines on the bill.
* **Outside your state** → the whole rate is **IGST 5%**. One line on the bill.

They never both appear on the same line. It is not "IGST and State GST" — it is
"CGST+SGST **or** IGST", and one bill line is one or the other.

For a union territory without its own legislature — Chandigarh, Lakshadweep,
Andaman & Nicobar, Ladakh, Dadra & Nagar Haveli and Daman & Diu — the second
half is called **UTGST**, not SGST. Delhi, Puducherry and Jammu & Kashmir have
legislatures, so they use SGST. **The app has no idea UTGST exists.** A shop in
Chandigarh prints the wrong word on every bill.

### 1.2 "A restaurant chain in many states pays tax to the centre, a single-state restaurant pays differently"

**This is not how it works, and it is the most important correction in this
document.**

A chain does **not** charge IGST because it is a chain. GST registration is
**state-wise**. A chain with outlets in Karnataka, Maharashtra and Delhi holds
**three separate GSTINs** — one per state, all on the same PAN. Each outlet is
legally a **distinct person**.

And every one of those outlets charges **CGST + SGST** on its own customers'
bills. All three. Always. A 200-outlet chain and a one-room darshini bill
customers identically.

The reason is **Section 12(4) of the IGST Act**: for restaurant and catering
service, the place of supply is **where the service is actually performed** —
the table the food is served at. A customer who flew in from Delhi and eats in
your Bengaluru restaurant is an **intra-state** supply, because the food was
served in Bengaluru. Their home state is irrelevant.

**So a sit-down restaurant can essentially never charge IGST on a customer
bill.**

But your instinct that a multi-state chain is different **is not wrong** — it is
just about a different thing:

| The real multi-state difference | Does the app handle it? |
|---|---|
| One GSTIN and one state code **per outlet** | **Yes** — `store_profile` is keyed by `outlet_id` (`0001_initial.sql:58`). The plumbing is correct. |
| An outlet picker so a chain can manage them | **No** — Settings edits one profile and there is no outlet UI. |
| Moving stock between states = **IGST**, tax invoice, e-way bill | **No** — `crates/mb-core/src/transfer.rs` carries no tax at all. |
| Outdoor catering performed in another state = IGST | **No** — cannot be set per bill. |

**This is where IGST genuinely lives in your product, and all three are
missing.** IGST is for *moving goods between your own branches across a state
line* and for *catering an event in another state* — never for a walk-in
customer.

## 2. How restaurant tax actually works in India, as of today

Verified against sources, not memory. This is the target the app has to hit.

### 2.1 The slabs changed under you

**The 12% and 28% slabs were abolished on 22 September 2025** by the 56th GST
Council. The structure is now:

| Slab | What it is for |
|---|---|
| **0%** | Exempt / nil-rated — UHT milk, pre-packaged paneer, chena and much else moved here |
| **5%** | Restaurant service, and most food and daily-use goods |
| **18%** | The standard rate for everything else |
| **40%** | Sin and luxury goods |

Plus special rates (0.25%, 1.5%, 3%) for gold, diamonds and jewellery, which a
restaurant will never touch.

**Your app still ships the old world.** `crates/mb-core/src/tax.rs:34-36` defines
`GST_12` and `GST_28` as named constants, `starting_classes()`
(`taxclass.rs:134`, the 12% entry at `:144`) seeds every new shop with
**"Packaged goods 12%"**, and the first-run setup wizard offers the owner **"Packaged 12%"** as a choice. A shop
set up on your app today starts with a tax class that no longer legally exists.

This is exactly your point about not hardcoding slabs — and it has *already*
bitten. The rate must be **free entry**, and the seeds must be current.

### 2.2 What rate a restaurant charges

| Situation | Rate |
|---|---|
| Standalone restaurant — dine-in, parcel, own delivery | **5%, no input credit** |
| Restaurant inside a hotel with room tariff **above ₹7,500/night** | **18%, with input credit** |
| Composition scheme restaurant | **5% paid by the shop**, nothing charged to the customer |

**Takeaway is not different from dine-in.** Parcel from a restaurant is still
restaurant service at 5%. This matters, because the app carries a feature built
on the opposite belief (§3.5).

### 2.3 Why your objection about packaged goods was right — and beat my own recommendation

I proposed one shop-wide switch for "prices include tax". You said a shop selling
differently-taxed goods cannot use one setting. **You were right, and here is the
proof I should have led with.**

**MRP is inclusive of all taxes, by law.** A ₹20 bottle of water sold at MRP is
₹20 all-in — the tax is already inside it and must be worked backwards out.
Meanwhile the same shop's ₹100 dosa is ₹100 **plus** 5% = ₹105.

So the same bill legitimately carries:

```
MASALA DOSE     100.00   + 5% on top      (exclusive)
WATER 1L         20.00   tax already in   (inclusive, MRP)
BEER            250.00   outside GST      (VAT, see §2.4)
```

**One shop-wide switch cannot express that bill.** Inclusive-vs-exclusive has to
live with the item, exactly like the rate. My recommendation was wrong and is
withdrawn.

(A footnote worth knowing: a 2017 Supreme Court ruling lets hotels and
restaurants charge **above** MRP for packaged water, because serving it is part
of a service. So "MRP means inclusive" is the shop's choice to make, not a rule
the app should force.)

### 2.4 Liquor — the gap you told me not to skip

You said: *"How should I know this? do proper research and implement best. Do not
skip just because it needs more coding."* Fair. Here is the answer.

**Alcohol is outside GST by the Constitution itself** (Article 366(12A)). But it
is emphatically **not untaxed**. It attracts **state excise and state VAT**, at
rates that are not small:

| State | VAT on liquor |
|---|---|
| Karnataka | **20%** |
| Maharashtra | **25%** country liquor, **35%** foreign liquor |

Other states differ again, and some cities add a municipal cess on top (Mumbai,
Pune).

**Your app charges zero.** `TaxTreatment::NonGst` computes no tax at all
(`tax.rs:189`). The tax class is even
named "Liquor — outside GST", and `taxclass.rs` says in a comment that this is
*"the one that matters commercially… why a bar could not use v1"*.

**A bar in Karnataka running Magic Bill undercharges every drink by 20%.** That
is the single most expensive defect in this audit. The app believes "outside GST"
means "no tax". It means "a different tax, which we do not compute".

What is needed is a **second tax channel** that is not GST:

* it has its own rate, per item, set by the shop (VAT differs by state and by
  liquor type);
* it prints as its **own line** on the bill — not merged into CGST/SGST;
* it is **excluded from GSTR-1 and every GST report**;
* it feeds a **separate liquor sales register**, because excise officers inspect
  that separately from the GST books.

Two parallel revenue ledgers on one bill is the legal shape, and the app
currently has one.

### 2.5 The service charge trap

The app defaults service charge to **off** (`service_charge_bp: 0`) — correct and
well judged. But if a shop turns it on, the app taxes it at **18%**
(`service_charge_tax_bp: 1_800`, `settings/mod.rs:295`).

Both halves of that are wrong now:

* The **CCPA guidelines of 4 July 2022**, upheld by the **Delhi High Court on 28
  March 2025**, say a service charge may not be added automatically or by
  default, and specifically that it **must not be added to the food bill with GST
  levied on the total**.
* If a customer genuinely volunteers it, it is part of the restaurant service —
  so **5%**, the same as the food. Never 18%.

The 18% default is a number from before the rate rationalisation that nobody
revisited.

### 2.6 What a bill must legally carry

Rule 46 of the CGST Rules lists 16 mandatory fields. The app does well here —
this is checked in §4 — but three are worth naming now:

* **HSN/SAC digits by turnover**: up to ₹5 crore → 4 digits on B2B, optional on
  B2C. Above ₹5 crore → 6 digits, mandatory. Restaurant service is **SAC 996331**.
* **Composition declaration**: a bill of supply must carry *"composition taxable
  person, not eligible to collect tax on supplies"*.
* **E-invoicing** above ₹5 crore turnover — B2B only, with an IRN and a signed QR
  code. Out of scope for a counter POS today, but it is the next threshold.

## 3. What is wrong in the app

Ordered by how much it costs a real shop.

### 3.1 ⛔ A bar undercharges every drink

**Where:** `crates/mb-core/src/tax.rs:189`, `crates/mb-core/src/taxclass.rs:144`

`NonGst` produces zero tax. Liquor needs state VAT at 20–35%. Covered in §2.4.

**Cost:** the shop pays the VAT out of its own margin, or fails an excise
inspection. On a bar doing ₹50,000 of liquor a day in Karnataka, that is
**₹10,000 a day**.

### 3.2 ⛔ A composition shop prints an illegal bill

**Where:** `crates/mb-print/src/template/bill.rs:300`, and the absence of
`is_composition` anywhere in `mb-core`.

`is_composition` changes the title to **"BILL OF SUPPLY"** — and nothing else.
The bill pipeline never sees the flag. So the document prints:

```
              BILL OF SUPPLY
...
CGST 2.5%                        2.50
SGST 2.5%                        2.50
```

A bill of supply that shows tax is a contradiction in law. A composition dealer
**may not collect or display GST at all**.

Three more composition rules the app does not know:

* a composition dealer **may not make inter-state supplies** — but the app will
  happily let one set "Where you usually supply: outside my state";
* a composition dealer **may not serve alcohol** — but the liquor tax class is
  seeded for every shop;
* the mandatory declaration exists as `receipt.composition_note`, but it is
  **free text that can be blank**, and the template prints nothing when it is
  (`bill.rs:772`).

### 3.3 ⛔ There is no unregistered-shop mode

A shop below the registration threshold (₹40 lakh goods / ₹20 lakh services, half
that in special category states) has **no GSTIN and must charge no GST**.

The app handles this only by accident: with no GSTIN it prints no title
(`bill.rs:300`). But nothing stops the tax being computed and printed. A brand
new shop that adds items before entering a GSTIN gets tax lines on a bill it has
no right to issue.

**There is no such thing as "what kind of taxpayer am I" in this app.** That is
the missing idea at the centre of the whole tax design.

### 3.4 🔴 Two settings that do nothing — the conflict you found

**Where:** `src-tauri/src/settings/mod.rs:425,427`; `catalog.rs:746,749`

* `tax.default_class_id` — *"Which tax class the Menu screen offers first."* The
  Menu screen actually uses `classes[0]` (`ui/src/menu/Menu.tsx:184`), the first
  in the list. The setting is ignored.
* `tax.prices_include_tax` — *"On: a 100 rupee dosa is 100 rupees on the bill."*
  Nothing reads it. The real decision is `TaxClass.treatment`, set on the Menu
  screen.

The second one is worse than dead: it **claims to decide the same thing** as the
tax class's treatment. An owner who turns it on and sees no change on the bill
has been lied to by their own settings screen.

**This is precisely the conflict you spotted. Delete both.**

### 3.5 🔴 A whole tax feature is built, stored, and never runs

**Where:** `crates/mb-core/src/taxclass.rs:67`, `crates/mb-db/src/repo/taxclass.rs:171`

`TaxClass` carries `by_order_type` — a different rate for dine-in vs parcel vs
delivery. It is modelled in core, given a database table (`tax_class_rates`),
written by `TaxClassRepo::save`, and read back by `TaxClassRepo::resolve`.

**`resolve` has no callers.** Not one, in the whole repository. And there is no
UI to set an override. The billing path takes the item's own denormalised rate
(`menu.rs:73`), which is always the class's **base** rate.

So the feature is dead weight — and worse, the reason it was built is **no longer
correct law**. Its own comment says *"some states tax the same dish differently
to take away"*. Takeaway from a restaurant is restaurant service at 5%, same as
dine-in.

**Recommendation: delete it.** Both the model and the table. It is a rule that
cannot fire, justified by a belief that is wrong.

### 3.6 🔴 A shop-wide setting that prints illegal bills

**Where:** `catalog.rs:741`, `settings/mod.rs:242`

*"Where you usually supply"* — intra or inter — is applied to **every bill**
(`billing.rs:133`, `flows.rs:942`). Set it to "inter" and every dine-in bill
charges IGST. As §1.2 established, that is illegal for restaurant service.

The help text makes it worse. It says: *"A single bill can still be changed."*
**There is no way to change a single bill.** I searched the whole UI — no
place-of-supply control exists on any screen. The tooltip describes a feature
that was never built.

### 3.7 🟡 The rate lives in three places, and one is a stale copy

The rate for an item is stored:

1. on the **tax class** (`tax_classes.rate_bp`);
2. **copied onto the item** (`items.tax_rate_bp`, `items.tax_treatment`);
3. **frozen onto the bill line** when it is billed.

(3) is correct and is a genuine strength — crown jewel 4, old bills never change.

(2) is a deliberate denormalisation, and `TaxClassRepo::save` *does* rewrite it
when a class changes (`repo/taxclass.rs:152`). Good.

**But it copies only the base rate**, so if the order-type overrides in §3.5 were
ever wired up, the copy would silently be wrong. The two features are already
incompatible. Deleting §3.5 removes the trap.

### 3.8 🟡 Hardcoded obsolete slabs

Covered in §2.1. `GST_12` and `GST_28` are named constants; "Packaged goods 12%"
is seeded into every new shop; the setup wizard offers 12%.

Per your instruction — the app must not hardcode slabs at all. `TaxRate` already
takes **any** basis points from 0 to 10000 and validates the range
(`tax.rs:41`), which is the right foundation. Only the *convenience constants*
and the *seed list* are stale.

### 3.9 🟡 Service charge at 18%

Covered in §2.5.

### 3.10 🟡 No UTGST

Covered in §1.1. A shop in Chandigarh, Lakshadweep, Andaman & Nicobar, Ladakh or
Dadra & Nagar Haveli prints "SGST" where the law says "UTGST".

`PlaceOfSupply` has exactly two values, `Intra` and `Inter` (`tax.rs:109`), and
the print template hardcodes the word "SGST" (`bill.rs:716`).

### 3.11 🟡 HSN is unvalidated free text

`hsn: Option<String>` with no digit-count rule and no link to turnover. There is
no SAC for the restaurant service itself (996331). The bill can print a 2-digit
HSN for a shop that legally needs 6.

### 3.12 🔵 "Composition" means two different things

`ui/src/menu/Composition.tsx` is about **what a dish is made of** — sizes,
choices, combos. `store.is_composition` is the **GST composition scheme**. Two
unrelated ideas, one word, in one app. Rename one before somebody wires them
together by mistake.

## 4. What is right, and must not be broken

This audit is mostly bad news, so it is worth being precise about how much of the
foundation is genuinely good. **The engine is well built. The wiring around it is
not.**

| What | Where | Why it is right |
|---|---|---|
| Rates as **basis points**, never floats | `tax.rs:29` | 2.5% is exact. A float would drift. |
| Inclusive tax by **remainder, not second rounding** | `tax.rs:205` | Guarantees `taxable + tax == menu price` exactly. Property-tested over 2,000 amounts × 4 rates (`tax.rs:357`). |
| `halve_exact` for the CGST/SGST split | `tax.rs:213` | CGST + SGST equals the whole tax to the paisa. Tested over 3,000 amounts. |
| **Discount before tax** | `bill.rs:274-290` | Section 15(3)(a). Tax on money the customer never paid would be wrong and the app gets it right. |
| **Frozen snapshots** on every line | `menu.rs:73` | Changing a rate today cannot rewrite yesterday's bill. |
| Exempt and non-GST kept **apart** | `tax.rs:85` | They are different in a return. Most small POS software merges them. |
| Rate-wise summary + HSN summary | `repo/reports.rs:371,415` | What GSTR-1 is actually built from. |
| **GSTIN checked against state code** | `catalog.rs:1262` | Catches a Kerala GSTIN on a Karnataka shop. Almost nobody does this check. |
| Round-off as its own recorded figure | `bill.rs:389` | The printed lines always sum to the printed total. |
| Charges carry **their own** rate | `charge.rs:46` | A charge taxed differently from the food is a real case. |
| Tax invoice vs bill of supply title | `bill.rs:300` | The right idea — it just needs the rest of §3.2. |
| One `store_profile` **per outlet** | `0001_initial.sql:58` | The multi-state chain model is already correct in the database. |

**The pipeline order is fixed and numbered in the code** (`bill.rs`, steps 1–8),
with a comment saying that reordering it changes what a customer pays and what a
CA files. That is the right way to protect a money path.

## 5. The design that removes the confusion

The fix is not to delete tax from Settings. It is to **give Settings and the Menu
different jobs and never let them overlap.**

### 5.1 Settings owns WHO THE SHOP IS

One new idea replaces the scattered flags — **what kind of taxpayer is this
shop?**

```
Tax registration:
  ( ) Not registered        — no GSTIN, no tax on any bill, plain bill
  ( ) Composition scheme    — GSTIN, no tax shown, BILL OF SUPPLY + declaration
  (•) Regular GST           — GSTIN, tax shown, TAX INVOICE
```

Choosing one **decides behaviour, not just a label**:

| | Not registered | Composition | Regular |
|---|---|---|---|
| GSTIN required | no | yes | yes |
| Tax computed on a bill | **never** | **never** | yes |
| Bill title | none | BILL OF SUPPLY | TAX INVOICE |
| Declaration printed | no | **yes, and not blankable** | no |
| Liquor allowed | yes | **no — refuse it** | yes |
| Inter-state allowed | n/a | **no — refuse it** | yes |

Everything else on the tax screen is shop identity too: GSTIN, state code, and
whether the shop is a hotel restaurant above ₹7,500 (which changes the default
rate offered to 18% with credit).

**No rate ever appears on this screen.**

### 5.2 The Menu owns WHAT THINGS ARE

The tax class stays exactly where it is, and keeps both of its jobs:

* the **rate** — free entry in percent, any value, no hardcoded slab list;
* the **kind** — one of four, which is a legal category, not a rate:

| Kind | Meaning | Goes in GSTR-1? |
|---|---|---|
| Taxed | Normal GST at the class rate | yes, with taxable value and tax |
| Exempt | Nil-rated | yes, as exempt value |
| Outside GST | Liquor — **carries its own VAT rate** | **no**, separate register |
| Non-taxable | Genuinely no tax of any kind | no |

Plus, per class, the pricing convention you were right about:

* **price is tax-on-top** (the dosa), or
* **price already includes tax** (the MRP water bottle).

### 5.3 The one change that makes §2.4 possible

`TaxTreatment` today mixes two unrelated ideas into one enum: *inclusive vs
exclusive* (a pricing convention) and *exempt vs non-GST* (a legal category).
That is why liquor cannot have a rate — the moment you call it `NonGst`, the enum
has already decided it is inclusive-or-exclusive of nothing.

**Split it into two fields.** A kind, and a pricing convention. Then
"outside GST, VAT 20%, price already includes it" becomes expressible — and a bar
can be billed.

### 5.4 Place of supply becomes per-bill and rare

Delete the shop-wide default. Restaurant service is always intra-state. Add an
inter-state option only where it is real:

* on a **catering/event order**, where the event is in another state;
* on a **stock transfer** between outlets in different states, which needs IGST,
  a tax invoice and an e-way bill above ₹50,000.

## 6. The order I would build it in

Not a schedule — a dependency order. Each step is finishable on its own.

| # | What | Why here |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Delete the two dead settings** (§3.4) | Removes the conflict you reported. One hour's work, and the screen stops lying. |
| 2 | **Delete the order-type override** (§3.5) | Removes a trap before anything else touches tax classes. |
| 3 | **Taxpayer kind in Settings** (§5.1) | The missing idea everything else hangs off. Fixes §3.2 and §3.3 together. |
| 4 | **Split `TaxTreatment` into kind + pricing** (§5.3) | The enabling refactor. Touches core, db, print, UI — but nothing works properly until it is done. |
| 5 | **Liquor VAT** (§2.4) | The most expensive defect. Needs step 4 first. |
| 6 | **Place of supply per bill, not per shop** (§3.6) | Removes a way to print an illegal bill. |
| 7 | **Current slabs, free rate entry, reseed** (§2.1, §3.8) | Includes a migration for shops already carrying a 12% class. |
| 8 | **UTGST** (§3.10) | Small, self-contained. |
| 9 | **Service charge to 5% + a CCPA warning** (§2.5) | Small. |
| 10 | **HSN digit rules and SAC 996331** (§3.11) | Compliance polish. |
| 11 | **Rename menu "Composition"** (§3.12) | Cheap, and prevents a future mistake. |

Steps 1, 2 and 9 are small and safe. **Step 4 is the big one** and everything
after it depends on it.

## 7. What I still need from you

None of these block the plan above — they change details inside it.

1. **Do you want a "hotel restaurant at 18% with input credit" mode?** It is a
   different kind of customer (hotels, not standalone restaurants). If no, the
   default rate offered stays 5% and step 3 gets simpler.
2. **How far do you want liquor to go?** The minimum is VAT on the bill and a
   separate total. The full version is a liquor sales register that an excise
   officer can read, and daily stock reconciliation. The first is a week; the
   second is a feature in its own right.
3. **Do you want a GSTR-1 export file**, or is the on-screen rate-wise report
   enough for a CA to type from? The data is already there; only the export is
   missing.
4. **Should the app refuse an illegal combination, or warn?** Composition + liquor
   is not allowed by law. My recommendation is refuse, with a sentence saying
   why — but it is your product.
