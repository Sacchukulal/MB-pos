## D01 Dashboard sales and drawer explanation

### What was observed

Image 1 showed four bills, takings of 1,606.00, average bill of 401.50, and expected drawer cash of 955.00. The owner asked whether cash, card, and UPI explain the difference.

The actual database agreed with the screenshot:

| Payment mode | Bills using that mode in this example | Amount |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Cash | 2 | INR 955.00 |
| Card | 1 | INR 357.00 |
| UPI | 1 | INR 294.00 |
| Total | 4 | INR 1,606.00 |

`955 + 357 + 294 = 1,606`, and `1,606 / 4 = 401.50`. There were no cash movements, expenses, refunds, or customer credit collections in the queried day that explained an additional difference. This is a reconciled example, not evidence of an arithmetic bug.

### What the code means

`dashboard_on` reads the shared daily totals. `day_totals` calculates net sales as billed grand totals less voided grand totals. It is not simply adding every payment received. A credit sale can contribute to sales without physical cash arriving, and tips are not sales.

For a one-day period, the drawer card uses `cash_position`. Its expected value includes opening float, cash receipts and tips, cash collected against customer credit, and top-ups; it subtracts recorded cash refunds, cash expenses, payouts, bank drops, supplier cash payments, and cash still held by delivery riders. The dashboard currently asks for the whole shop's cash position; the drawer-count screen can ask for a particular till. Do not confuse those scopes during future multi-till work.

Voiding a bill cancels the sale but does not itself mean money was handed back. The cash query therefore retains receipts on voided orders and separately subtracts recorded cash refunds. This is intentional. Subtracting the void amount from drawer cash as well would be wrong.

For a multi-day period, the dashboard switches to a cash-taken measure rather than presenting a single physical drawer balance. Archived daily summaries also have explicit limits on which detailed figures can be shown.

### Problem and proposed change

The terms "Takings" and "In the drawer" are easy to read as two versions of the same number. Prefer wording such as "Net sales" and "Expected cash in drawer", with a short explanation or existing breakdown access. Keep actual counted cash distinct from expected cash.

Do not change the underlying formula merely to make the cards match. If another example fails reconciliation, trace its bill totals, receipts, refunds, movements, business day, and terminal scope before proposing a financial fix.

## B01 Individual bill access and cashier permissions

### Confirmed problem

The default cashier role includes `BillRevert` and `BillReprint`, but does not include `ReportsView`. Both `bills_on` and `bill_detail_on` require `ReportsView`. The Reports UI also hides the Bills entry when report permission is denied.

Consequently, a cashier may have permission to perform an operation yet be unable to find and open the bill through the screen that exposes it. Granting report permission to solve this would reveal information that is unrelated to ordinary bill work.

The same structural problem affects license handling: bill operations currently live inside Reports, but the owner wants individual bill work to remain available while reporting is locked.

### Proposed core solution

Define a clear permission boundary for finding and reading an individual bill. Reuse an existing suitable permission if there is one; otherwise add one shared permission with deliberate migration behavior for existing roles. Do not grant voiding or refunding rights to every cashier automatically. Keep those action permissions and any configured approval requirements separate.

Provide a direct operational Bills entry or equivalent access from Billing. It may reuse the existing list/detail component, but its response must not bundle paid aggregate reports or unrestricted exports. Coordinate the server response design with L01; hiding totals in React while returning them over IPC does not establish the intended boundary.

Searching for an individual receipt must remain practical. Preserve useful number, date, and item lookup without assuming that "individual bill work" authorizes unrestricted downloadable history. Any limit on browsing or pagination should support the confirmed requirement rather than making old bills impossible to find.

## B02 Full refunds and partial item returns

### Confirmed problems

In `Bills.tsx`, `actionsFor` exposes "Give money back" for a non-paid bill after the paid bill has been voided. The `Correction` component sends `amountPaise: Number(bill.total.paise)` and `mode: 'cash'` to `refund_bill`. It does not ask which amount or payment method was actually returned.

For example, a customer paid INR 357 by card. If the operator returns INR 357 through the card provider and records the return through this screen, POS records cash going out. The expected cash drawer becomes INR 357 too low. Recording cash would only be correct if cash was actually handed back; the screen currently gives no choice.

The refund repository also requires the order to be voided. It limits cumulative ordinary refunds against the stored payments, which is a useful safeguard, but does not make this a complete item-return flow. The UI always resends the full bill total rather than the remaining refundable amount after an earlier partial refund. That can lead to a refusal even when a smaller remaining refund would be valid.

Partial item returns currently use the bill-edit path instead. `complete_bill_with_returns_on` calculates the difference, requires the returned amounts to add up, and uses `Settlement::return_to` to validate allocation against payment modes. This is stronger than the full-refund screen. Do not discard these checks when unifying the experience.

### Proposed core solution

Offer explicit actions on one bill: edit, return selected items or quantities, and void the whole bill. Before confirmation, show the original receipts, previous returns, proposed sales adjustment, remaining refundable amount, and actual refund method. Prefill a sensible single-method amount when unambiguous, but let the operator confirm what happened.

Use shared Rust validation for both whole-bill and partial returns. Reuse the existing money, settlement, order, and correction repositories. Do not keep one permissive cash-only refund implementation alongside a different strict correction implementation.

For each operation, distinguish the sales adjustment from money movement. A refund record does not itself call a bank, card gateway, or UPI provider in the audited flow. Do not show "refunded" in a way that claims an external payment was executed when POS only recorded it.

Handle mixed cash/card/UPI receipts, previous refunds, credit settlements, and tips explicitly. In particular, a credit entry is not proof that cash was received. Decide whether cross-method refunds are allowed, and enforce the same rule everywhere. The existing correction path restricts returns to original payment modes; preserve that until a deliberate policy change is approved.

Keep order state, refund records, stock or cost effects, audit entries, and sync changes consistent. Use a request identity or equivalent established mechanism so retrying the same confirmation cannot refund twice. Reject unsupported payment methods and invalid amounts in Rust, not only in the UI.

## B03 Bill editing and approval flow

### Existing safeguards to preserve

`revert_bill_on` keeps the issued sale intact while saving a working correction. It keeps the bill number, saves the earlier lines and payments in the correction history, and checks whether the active counter has unsaved work before replacing its cart. Completing the bill commits the replacement through the existing settlement path.

Line removal and quantity reduction include checks for stale line selection and changes from another device. Removing food already sent to the kitchen requires the relevant permission and a reason. A failed print is distinct from failure to record the bill. These safeguards solve real problems and must survive UI simplification.

### Confirmed usability problems

Editing starts in Reports, opens a correction reason dialog, moves the bill back to Billing, and requires completion there. A reduced bill can then open a separate return dialog. Finishing does not by itself satisfy the separate correction sign-off mechanism.

`approve_if_needed` checks the configured threshold when a void or revert is submitted. The UI first submits without an approver, then expands the dialog after `void.needs_approval`. This creates a predictable failed attempt before the user sees a required field.

There are two approval concepts: permission/threshold approval before an operation, and `approve_revert` as later review of a completed correction. Do not remove one without deciding whether they represent distinct business controls. Equally, do not require both merely because the current screens happen to expose both.

### Additional edge cases to verify before redesign

Investigate removing every item from an issued correction. Completion rejects an empty bill; voiding refuses an unfinished correction; ordinary cancellation accepts an issued open order rather than a settled issued bill with a working correction. No explicit discard-correction action was identified during the audit. This combination suggests an awkward recovery path, but it was not exercised end to end and is not recorded as a reproduced data-loss bug.

Also check cancellation after an item-change print failure, leaving an unfinished correction, reopening it after restart, and phone changes arriving while an edit is in progress. Keep existing stale-update protection.

### Proposed core solution

Use one operational bill workspace with clear actions and a review step. Display original total, new total, amount to collect or return, and any required manager approval before confirmation. Fetch an authoritative action preview from Rust rather than reproducing threshold or refund rules in React. Revalidate on commit because another device or manager can change the state after preview.

Provide an explicit safe way to leave or discard a working correction while preserving the issued bill and audit policy. If removing all items means voiding and refunding, guide the user through that valid operation rather than leaving an empty unfinishable edit.

Reasons should use the shared reason mechanism. Do not add repeated dialogs for every click when one operation-level reason is sufficient; retain additional kitchen cancellation information where needed.

## B04 Returns against bills from closed days

### Confirmed restriction

`void_bill_on` checks the original bill's business day and refuses when that day is closed. `record_refund` only accepts a voided bill. `refund_on` separately checks the current refund day. Together these rules mean a return against a settled historical bill may first require reopening its original day and voiding it there.

Closed-day protection is valuable. Removing that protection globally would allow past totals to change unexpectedly. The problem is the lack of a straightforward current-day return operation linked to the historical sale.

### Proposed core solution

Design a return or adjustment record that references the original bill while recording the actual refund on the appropriate current business day. Preserve the original closed record and make the adjustment visible in both bill history and the relevant reporting period. Reuse existing correction/refund storage where it can represent this accurately; extend the model deliberately if it cannot.

Before implementation, settle the policy for sales/tax adjustments, stock disposition, and exceptional edits to closed bills. This audit does not establish a tax or statutory-document policy. Do not silently rewrite a closed day's sales or reopen it automatically to bypass an error.

### Acceptance checks

A permitted return against yesterday's closed bill has a clear workflow and a traceable link to the original. Current-day cash changes once and by the actual cash refunded. Historical closed totals remain unchanged unless an explicitly authorized historical adjustment is part of the selected policy. Reports explain adjustments without double-counting. If the current day is closed, the UI gives the appropriate operational action. Direct commands obey the same rules as the screen.

### Implementation prompt

> Implement B04 after checking B02's final model. Replace the need to reopen an old day merely to return money with a deliberate historical-bill return or adjustment path. Preserve closed-day integrity and link the return to its original bill. Resolve any material sales/tax posting decision with the owner before committing to that behavior. Do not weaken the existing closed-day guards globally. Test original and refund dates, cash and noncash modes, reporting, history, and repeated requests.

## L02 Expiry timing and already displayed reports

### Confirmed problem

`App::entitlement` returns a cached decision. The licensing refresher normally checks the cloud daily, retries failed checks hourly, and wakes at least hourly to reconsider the saved license. A date boundary can therefore pass while a previously allowed decision remains cached until the next reconsideration. Other actions may refresh it sooner, so this is a possible delay rather than a guaranteed full hour.

License notifications currently carry banner text and tone. The Shell updates the banner, but the mounted Dashboard and Reports components do not use that notification to invalidate their loaded data. A report already visible can remain on screen after the underlying entitlement changes. Their next protected request can fail, but that is not the same as promptly locking the displayed paid screen.

Remote suspension is a separate timing issue: a counter cannot know about a server-side status change while completely offline unless its signed offline grant already limits the period. This audit does not establish a requirement for instant remote revocation offline.

### Proposed core solution

Keep cloud access off the billing path. Calculate current local eligibility from the verified snapshot and current time whenever a protected feature is requested, or use a rigorously time-bounded shared cache. ## L03 Persisted emergency unlock trust

### Confirmed security problem

The normal snapshot is signed and verified. However, `LicenceFile` stores `emergency_until` as a separate plain serialized field outside that signed snapshot. `LicenceFile::load` deserializes it directly. `Licensing::entitlement` checks that field before normal machine-binding and snapshot-usability checks.

When the stored emergency time is in the future and a previously issued valid snapshot exists, the code creates an emergency entitlement using that snapshot's plan features. Thus a person able to edit the local license file can influence the unlock period without presenting a newly verified emergency authorization. This can bypass expiry for previously licensed features.

The normal command for redeeming an emergency code validates that code before saving the timestamp. The weakness is trusting the persisted result later without a verifiable grant. Signing the ordinary snapshot does not protect fields outside it.

No installed license file was altered to demonstrate this. The finding is established by the load and decision paths. This is not evidence that someone used the bypass on either screenshot's account.

### Proposed core solution

Persist a verifiable emergency grant, not an authoritative bare timestamp. Bind the grant to the intended license/shop and machine, its expiry, and its allowed scope. Validate its authenticity and binding each time it is used. Reuse the established asymmetric signature infrastructure where suitable; do not add a signing secret to the shipped POS binary.

Review emergency issuance and redemption across the POS crate and backend tool before selecting a new format. Any compatibility migration must fail safely for unverifiable old emergency state while keeping ordinary billing available. Do not silently convert an arbitrary old timestamp into a trusted new grant.

Inspect replay handling, expiry, clock rollback, and key rotation as part of the same trust boundary. Do not make unrelated protocol changes without a concrete need.

## N02 Arrival detection and timer defects

### Confirmed behavior and reproduced failure

`shape(tile)` in `arrivals.ts` returns only `tile.total?.text`. The hook keys that value by order ID. A new order ID or changed displayed total counts as fresh; other changes do not. An item substitution, note change, or offsetting quantity changes can leave the total unchanged and therefore produce no alert. Whether every such change should blink needs an explicit event definition; total-only comparison cannot represent that choice accurately.

The hook also starts one timeout per detected batch. If an order changes again before its earlier timeout ends, the old timeout still removes that order from the alert set. Keeping the same `mb-arrived` class on the element does not restart the CSS animation.

The browser check reproduced this: an update started the animation; another arrived about two seconds later; the animation retained its earlier running time; roughly 1.25 seconds after the second update, the alert class was gone. The second update did not receive a fresh three-second alert.

The first non-null floor list establishes a baseline and never animates. Leaving the screen clears timers. This explains why orders received while away from the screen may not blink on return; it is existing behavior, not automatically a bug under the clarified short-alert requirement.

Reduced-motion CSS sets the beat duration and count to zero. The hook consequently removes the alert immediately, and no alternative arrival marker is supplied by this mechanism. Preserve accessibility preferences, but do not lose important order information solely because motion is disabled.

### Proposed core solution

Define what merits an arrival alert: at minimum, a newly received order and the relevant new-item additions to an existing order. Separate this from timer ticks, selection changes, payment completion, and harmless list refreshes.

Prefer an authoritative order revision or suitable event sequence if the existing model provides one. Audit that field's semantics before reusing it; a revision that changes for every timer or irrelevant update will create false alerts. Do not generate a new ID or timestamp merely because the UI fetched the same data again.

Maintain the alert deadline or generation per order in the existing shared arrival tracker. A subsequent relevant update must restart that order's visual pulse and expiration. An older timeout must not clear a newer generation. Restart the visual animation deliberately without remounting the entire order card, moving keyboard focus, or resetting its controls.

Keep Billing, Floor, and Processing on the shared mechanism. Deduplicate the `floor` and `floorChanged` notifications for the same committed change so a single phone batch does not beep or pulse repeatedly. Avoid a second competing tracker in Shell or Floor.

Provide a brief nonanimated highlight or new-order marker when reduced motion is requested. Respect an explicit shop setting that disables arrival alerts. The owner has not asked for persistent blinking or an acknowledgement workflow; do not add either as a workaround.
